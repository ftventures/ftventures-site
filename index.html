Why late stage

We invest where the returns came from

Clearer leaders, further upside

By the later rounds the field has narrowed and the winner is visible. We buy before that lead is fully priced in.

A shorter path to liquidity

Entering closer to the IPO or sale means shorter holds. Cash comes back to investors in years, not a decade.

Fewer zeros

Real revenues and proven demand mean these companies rarely go to zero. The portfolio does not depend on one outsized winner.

Concentrate with confidence

Years of evidence show how consistently a company innovates, wins and grows. That record justifies fewer, larger positions.

What a decade taught me

Lessons learnt from a decade of investing

01

The biggest wins didn’t look obvious

Revolut was widely debated in 2017. I build conviction independently, not by following the crowd.

02

Late entry into a leader still compounds

Buying more Revolut at 16x the first entry price still returned 11x. A proven leader is worth paying up for.

03

Founders remember who moved fast

Raising as a founder at Lazare Farms showed me how rare a decisive investor is. We decide in days and close on the terms agreed.

04

Early stage was the weak spot

Most write-offs were early consumer and food brands. We invest once a leader is clear.
